Expedition Construction

Rules for building Your Expedition Roster and War Parties

  • Expedition Roster: 1500 points
  • War Party (army list per battle): 750 points

War Parties for each battle are selected from your Expedition Roster. These rules replace the standard Warhammer Fantasy army composition rules (such as percentage requirements for Lords, Heroes, Core, Special, and Rare). All other normal rules still apply, including character magic item limits, unit options and upgrades, mount availability, and any other restrictions listed in the army books.

Character Restrictions

  • Lord-level characters are not allowed
  • Named characters are not allowed

This campaign focuses on new characters created by the players. Your Expedition Leader and companions represent new adventurers seeking their fortune, not famous figures of the Warhammer world.

Players are encouraged to name their Expedition Leader and companions, and give a short description of why they joined the expedition.

1. Create Your Expedition Leader

  • Up to 150 points
  • Must be a Hero-level character
  • Wizards must be Level 1

Monstrous Infantry Leaders: If your Expedition Leader is Monstrous Infantry, the point limit increases to 175 points.

Mounts: Mounts must be purchased during Expedition Roster creation. The cost of the mount does not count toward the character's personal point limit, but does count toward the total Expedition Roster limit.

Mundane Equipment: Mundane Equipment is not counted toward your Expedition Roster — it is purchased when building a War Party for each scenario.

Your Expedition Leader counts toward your Expedition Roster.

Companions

Your Expedition Leader is not alone on this journey.

Additional characters and unit champions in your Expedition Roster are considered companions. These individuals may take command of the War Party in battles where your Expedition Leader is not present.

Players are encouraged to name their companions and describe why they follow the Expedition Leader.

Hero's Journey

Your Expedition Leader must take part in the first and final battles of the campaign.

In other scenarios they may rest, allowing a companion to lead the War Party.

2. Create Your Expedition Roster

Create a 1500 point Expedition Roster including your Expedition Leader.

  • Up to 2 additional Characters may be included
    • These follow the same construction rules as your Expedition Leader
  • Minimum 2 Infantry units
    • Each must contain 10+ models
  • Maximum 25 models per unit
  • Maximum 225 points per model
  • Up to 2 War Machines, but they must be different types
  • Maximum one Chariot
  • Maximum one Monster
  • Up to 75 points of faction magic items
  • Up to 300 points spent on characters + magic items
Unique or unusual units should be classified by their closest equivalent, using common sense and table agreement.

3. Building a War Party

For each scenario, select units from your Expedition Roster to create a 750 point War Party.

War Parties are constructed following Warhammer Fantasy rules, with the following additional restrictions:

  • At least 1 character
  • At least 2 non-character units
  • Maximum 2 Special choices
  • Maximum 1 Rare choice
  • At least 1 infantry unit with 10+ models

If the unit fulfilling this requirement is Monstrous Infantry, the minimum size is 3 models.

Equipment: When building your War Party, you may purchase mundane equipment for characters and units as normal.

4. Unit Size

  • Units do not need to be fielded at full size
  • Units may be split into two smaller units if their size allows
  • If split, divide command models between them

Campaign Rules

Core rules for running the expedition campaign

Glory

Your expedition's progress is measured in Glory.

Players gain Glory by:

  • Winning battles
  • Completing scenario objectives

Glory represents reputation, discoveries, and momentum in the race for the Jewel of Lustria.

Fatigue

The expedition is long and exhausting.

No unit may participate in three scenarios in a row.

After fighting in two consecutive battles, the unit must sit out the next scenario.

Heroes and companions are exempt.

If a player must break this rule, the unit must roll on the Misbehaving Mercenaries table before the battle.

Determination

Defeat steels the resolve of an expedition determined to continue the hunt.

Players gain Determination based on campaign losses.

Losses Determination
0 0
1 1
2-3 2
4+ 3

Determination refreshes each battle.

A point may be spent to:

  • Reroll a single D6 or D3 Die
  • Reroll a Scatter Die
    • You cannot score a Hit! result on the reroll
  • Reroll a non-misfired Artillery Die
  • Grant your Hero or Companion a 4+ Ward Save (once)
  • Perform an extra dispel attempt (no wizard bonus)

Loot

Some scenarios award Loot. For each Loot gained, roll 2D6 to determine what category of item was discovered.

2-3 Insignia
4-5 Weapon
6-8 Consumable
9-10 Armour
11-12 Artifact

The full Loot Table with specific items appears in the Loot section.

Poor Man's Coin

After the third scenario, any player who has not yet gained Loot may make one free roll on the Loot Table.

Pirate Traders

Pirates and smugglers along the Lustrian coast are always willing to trade.

Between scenarios, players may exchange magical curiosities through these traders.

Two magical items may be traded for one faction magic item worth up to 50 points.

Sworn Enemies

Rivalries quickly become personal.

A Hero or Companion who was defeated in a challenge may designate the victor as their Sworn Enemy.

The model gains Hatred against that enemy.

Retreat Order

A commander may withdraw from battle.

During the Command Phase, a player may declare a Retreat Order.

All units immediately break and flee. Units in combat flee as normal and opponents may pursue.

Lustrian Terrain

Unique features and hazards of the jungle continent

Jungle Terrain

All Jungle terrain counts as Forest terrain, but with the following additional rules:

  • Jungle terrain provides Hard Cover.
  • Units making Dangerous Terrain tests in a jungle fail on a roll of 5+.
  • The following rules apply to all units except those with the Fly, Strider, or Skirmisher special rules- or those from the Lizardmen or Amazon army lists.
    • Units cannot March into, out of, or within Jungle terrain.
    • Charges are resolved using 1D6 instead of 2D6.
    • Swiftstride confers no benefit when charging, fleeing, pursuing, or overrunning into, out of, or within Jungle terrain.
    • Units must take Dangerous Terrain tests when fleeing, pursuing, or overrunning into, out of, or within Jungle terrain.

Narrative Overview

A Gift from the Stars

The story of the comet establishes the premise for the Jewel of Lustria campaign.

The Comet

Across the world, a blazing star was seen streaking through the heavens.

In the Empire, priests claimed they saw the sacred twin tail of Sigmar burning in the sky. In darker corners of the world, sorcerers whispered of warpstone falling from the firmament. Skaven seers shrieked with religious ecstasy at the arrival of a vast God-Stone. Ogre prophets declared that a second Maw had descended from the heavens.

Then the star vanished beyond the western horizon.

Within weeks, word spread among merchants, captains, and explorers: the comet had fallen somewhere deep within Lustria. Whether holy relic, divine omen, or unimaginable source of warpstone, the object quickly gained a name among those who sought it: the Jewel of Lustria.

The Expedition

Each player commands a small expedition gathered to pursue the Jewel of Lustria. These are not full armies, but warbands of adventurers, mercenaries, hardened soldiers, strange specialists, and ambitious leaders.

Many other expeditions roam the region as well. Smaller treasure-hunting parties, hired scouts, rival scholars, jungle hunters, and opportunists all crowd the same frontier. The deeper one travels into Lustria, the more inevitable conflict becomes.

Narrative Overview

Act I: The World Arrives in Lustria

Three scenarios compose the first act wherein expeditioners arrive and minor skirmishes break out. The adventurers have not yet learned to hate or respect each other as rivals.

Scenario I — Landing in the Green Hell

The expeditions arrive along the Lustrian coast within days of one another. Ships anchor along narrow beaches while scouts push cautiously into the jungle. Supplies are unloaded, camps established, and guides hired.

Rival expeditions encounter each other almost immediately as they attempt to secure footholds and begin the march inland. Small clashes erupt over landing sites, provisions, and intelligence.

These battles represent early skirmishes between competing expeditions establishing themselves along the coast.

Scenario II — Star Charts of the Old Ones

Lustria is vast and unfamiliar to most outsiders. Finding the exact site of the comet's impact by guesswork alone would take far too long.

Fortunately, the ancient Lizardmen once built observatories throughout the region to track celestial events. Within these ruined structures are star charts and astronomical records that may reveal the comet's true path.

Expeditions raid abandoned temple ruins in search of these charts, fighting among broken observatories where previous raiders may have overlooked priceless records.

Parallel battles represent separate observatory ruins being searched at the same time. Victorious expeditions gain the clearest path inland.

Scenario III — Crossing the Jade Vein

Guided by the star charts, the expeditions push deeper into the jungle until they reach a massive river known among explorers as the Jade Vein.

This immense waterway cuts across the region for miles in both directions. Marching around it would delay an expedition so badly that rivals would almost certainly reach the Jewel first.

The only option is to secure a crossing. Ancient bridges, broken causeways, and dangerous fords become the sites of bitter clashes as rival warbands struggle for passage deeper inland.

Parallel battles represent separate crossings along the same great river.

Narrative Overview

Act II: The Escalation

The expeditions are now in direct conflict. The free-for-all in Scenario IV leads to alliances being formed in the desperate race to find the Comet.

Scenario IV — The Tyrant of Xhotl-Tlan

Beyond the Jade Vein lies a feared stretch of jungle known as Xhotl-Tlan, the Valley of the Ancient Tyrant. There prowls an ancient Dread Saurian known to local hunters as K'thul-Xah, the Last Devourer.

For generations the beast has ruled the valley, destroying hunting parties, caravans, and expeditions alike. Even the best jungle trackers refuse to guide travelers through its domain while it still lives.

Now, with many armed expeditions gathered in the region, the hunters of the valley see their chance. Each has sworn an oath to personally slay the beast, though none could hope to do so alone.

They offer a bargain: help them kill the tyrant, and they will guide the expeditions onward through the most dangerous reaches of the jungle. They will continue to travel with the expedition afterward as navigators, though not as fighters.

The killing blow matters deeply to them. Pride, oath, and glory all demand that they—or the expedition favored by them—be credited with the monster's death. Though they will honor their agreement regardless, they promise greater rewards to the warband that grants them the most glorious outcome.

In addition to the main expeditions, smaller mercenary companies and lesser treasure-hunters converge on the hunt, creating a chaotic struggle around the beast.

Scenario V — Locating the Crater

After the tyrant falls, the expeditions press on. The star charts have guided them as far as they can, but the dense jungle canopy and broken terrain still conceal the exact location of the Jewel.

Different expeditions pursue different methods to find the impact site. Some seek the highest shattered ridges created by the comet's fall. Others turn to ancient Lizardmen instruments, hoping old observatories can still reveal what the jungle hides.

Scenario V-A — The Shattered Ridge

The comet's descent violently reshaped the landscape. Ridges of broken stone rise above the jungle canopy, while landslides and shattered cliffs create a maze of narrow passes.

Warbands race through this broken terrain seeking the highest ridge from which the crater can finally be seen. Rival expeditions collide while forcing their way through the same rocky bottlenecks.

Scenario V-B — The Temple Observatory

Not all expeditions trust the shattered ridges alone. Some search for one of the surviving Lizardmen observatories, believing its celestial instruments can be turned downward to reveal the impact site with precision.

Deep in the jungle stands a ruined observatory whose upper terraces rise above the canopy. Within it are ancient lensing artifacts once used to track the comet in the heavens—artifacts that may still be used to locate it upon the earth.

Warbands struggle through ruined halls, overgrown courtyards, and broken terraces to seize control of the observatory and gain the final bearing toward the Jewel.

Narrative Overview

Act III: The Final Struggle

The expeditions have reached their final destination. The battle is fought in the heart of an ancient temple city. The alliances left standing fight a grand battle for control of the crater and the Jewel itself.

Scenario VI — The Crater City

At last the truth is revealed. The comet did not fall into empty jungle, but into the center of an ancient Lustrian city.

The magical architecture of the Old Ones absorbed much of the physical destruction, leaving the city's plazas, temples, and terraces eerily intact. Yet when the meteor struck, a violent wave of warpstone energy swept through the city and killed every living creature instantly.

Ashen silhouettes still mark where Lizardmen once stood. At the heart of the silent ruins lies a vast crater, and within it rests the Jewel of Lustria.

One alliance reaches the city first and fortifies the approaches around the crater. The others follow soon after. Ancient defenses, shattered walls, temple terraces, and the city's dense urban core become the stage for the final struggle.

Alliances are made out of necessity, not trust. When the battle begins, all know that whoever controls the crater will claim a prize capable of reshaping the fate of the world.

Scenarios

Scenario I: First Blood

Rival expeditions clash within hours of reaching the shores of Lustria

The voyage across the ocean was long, miserable, and filled with rumor. Sailors spoke of a blazing comet falling deep within the jungles of Lustria, of lost temple cities and ancient relics waiting to be claimed by those bold enough to seek them.

Now those rumors have drawn ships from every corner of the world. Merchant caravels, pirate vessels, war galleys, and creaking transports crowd a small natural harbor known to sailors simply as the Emerald Coast.

The landing itself is chaos. Supplies are scarce, guides are unreliable, and every expedition fears that another may gain a head start into the jungle.

Rival war parties push inland from the beaches, each seeking fresh water, food, and a defensible campsite before beginning the long march toward the comet's resting place.

In such tense circumstances, it takes very little for discipline to collapse. A shouted insult. A drawn blade. A musket fired too quickly.

Within moments, two expeditions find themselves forming battle lines across an open stretch of coastal ground, scarcely a stone's throw apart.

Neither side intended to fight today.

But now that weapons are drawn and tempers are high, neither expedition is willing to back down.

Scenario I: Rules

How to play First Blood

Battlefield

The battle takes place on a relatively open stretch of coastal ground just inland from the landing site.

Do not place any large terrain features. A few pieces of scatter terrain such as supply crates, abandoned wagons, rocks, or low vegetation may be placed to represent debris from the landing.

Use a 72" × 48" battlefield.

Deployment

This battle represents a sudden confrontation between rival expeditions marching inland from the coast.

Each player deploys within the center 24" of their long table edge, to a depth of 12".

This leaves a gap of only 14" between the two deployment zones.

The outermost 5" along each table edge is not used for deployment.

Deployment is made in secret. Players should use books, screens, or other barriers to conceal their deployment while placing units.

Once both sides have completed deployment, the barriers are removed and the battle begins.

First Turn

The player whose army contains the character with the highest Initiative takes the first turn.

If both players tie, roll off normally to determine who begins.

Game Length

The battle lasts for six turns, or until one army has been completely destroyed.

Victory Conditions

Determine victory using normal Warhammer Fantasy 8th Edition Victory Points.

If a player wins the battle by a margin of 500 Victory Points or more, this counts as a Decisive Victory.

A battle that ends in victory within the first two rounds also counts as a Decisive Victory, representing a rapid collapse of the opposing expedition.

Decisive Victory (enemy army destroyed) 2 Glory + 1 Loot
Victory 2 Glory
Draw 1 Glory

Scenario II: Star Charts of the Old Ones

Rival expeditions raid a Lizardmen village in search of celestial knowledge

The jungles of Lustria are vast, hostile, and deeply unfamiliar to most outsiders. Even with guides and rumor to aid them, the rival expeditions soon realize that simply wandering inland in search of the fallen comet would waste precious weeks and all but guarantee failure.

Fortunately, the servants of the Old Ones have long studied the heavens. Across the jungle lie hidden villages and encampments where Skink astrologers keep celestial records, tracing the movement of stars, comets, and geomantic currents through the land.

Somewhere among these records lie the clues needed to follow the comet's descent. Yet the star charts are not stored in towering observatories alone. Even humble jungle settlements may preserve such knowledge in shrines, storehouses, or the personal collections of Skink priests.

Before long, rival expeditions descend upon one such village. Some come as looters, some as scholars, and some simply because they know their enemies seek something valuable within.

The settlement is not wholly deserted. Scattered defenders remain, and the intruders quickly discover that ransacking a village building by building is slow, dangerous work. Huts must be stormed, defenders driven out, and the interiors searched while enemies close in from all sides.

By nightfall, the jungle village has become a battlefield of burning thatch, broken idols, and desperate looting.

Scenario II: Rules

How to play Star Charts of the Old Ones

Victory Condition

The player who successfully recovers the most Treasure tokens wins the battle.

The expedition that wins this scenario automatically discovers a Star Chart of the Old Ones among the plunder recovered after the battle.

Battlefield Setup

The battle takes place within a small Lizardmen village or encampment.

Place 4 buildings on the battlefield, with one building in each of four divisions in the space between deployment zones. These represent huts, shrines, storehouses, or other village structures.

Each building contains a hidden Treasure token.

In addition to the buildings, players should place terrain to represent the surrounding jungle and settlement. A good battlefield for this scenario typically includes 1-2 Mysterious Jungles, 1-3 walls or fences, and a small amount of scatter terrain such as crates, campfires, supply piles, carved idols, or broken relics.

The village should also contain a small jungle shrine or sacred idol. This terrain piece's effects may be treated as one of the following:

D6 Treat As
1 Elven Waystone
2 Wizard's Tower
3 Sigmarite Shrine
4 Altar of Khaine
5 Idol of Gork
6 Magic Circle

Players may choose the shrine effect freely if preferred rather than rolling.

Deployment

Armies deploy using standard Battleline deployment rules.

First Turn

Roll off as normal. The player that finished deploying first receives +1 to this roll.

Game Length

The battle lasts until all Treasure tokens have been recovered, or until all units of one player have been removed from the battlefield.

Hidden Defenders

The first time a building is assaulted or entered, reveal its defenders.

Roll D6 to determine how many defenders occupy the building.

In a normal battle, these defenders are Skinks.

Searching the Buildings

At the beginning of each player's turn, any unit garrisoning a building may search it for treasure.

Roll a D6 and add the result to that building's running search total. When the total reaches 8 or more, a Treasure token is discovered.

Remove the building's treasure marker and place a Treasure token next to the unit that found it.

The search total belongs to the building, not the unit. If another unit captures the building later, it continues searching from the current total.

Occupied by Searching

A unit that searches a building that turn is considered fully occupied ransacking the structure and may not shoot or cast spells.

Characters garrisoning the building alongside a unit that is doing the searching may still act normally. Their followers perform the menial labor while they focus on the battle.

If a unit that is searching (chose to search at the start of their last turn) is engaged in close combat, it suffers -1 Initiative as the fighters scramble to drop their loot and arm themselves.

Transporting the Treasure

Treasure tokens move with the units carrying them.

If the unit carrying a Treasure token makes a Flee move, it drops the token at the start of its movement.

A dropped token may be picked up by any unit that comes into contact with it, but that unit must immediately end its movement.

If a unit carrying a token is destroyed in close combat, the victorious unit automatically takes control of the token.

Recovering the Treasure

A unit carrying a Treasure token that reaches any table edge may leave the battlefield. Any tokens it was carrying are considered successfully recovered.

Star Chart of the Old Ones

Enchanted Item - 25 points

These ancient charts record celestial movements and geomantic alignments observed by the servants of the Old Ones. Those who study them too closely may briefly glimpse the hidden patterns of the heavens.

At the start of each battle, the expedition possessing the chart must decide how it will be used.

The chart may either be carried into battle as an Enchanted Item, in which case the bearer rolls a D6 at the start of the battle to randomly determine a spell from the Lore of Heavens. Once per battle, the bearer may cast that spell as a Bound Spell (Power Level 5).

Alternatively, the expedition may consult the chart before battle instead of bringing it onto the field. If they do so, they may add or subtract 1 from their roll to determine who goes first. A chart used in this way confers no magical benefit during the battle itself.

Lizardmen Variant

If a Lizardmen player takes part in this scenario, they are not raiding for knowledge, but reclaiming sacred records stolen or disturbed by earlier intruders.

In such battles, the buildings are occupied not by Skinks, but by the remnants of previous raiders and treasure-hunters, represented by Dogs of War.

Scenario III: Crossing the Jade Vein

Rival expeditions battle for a vital bridge while daring companions chase treasure across deadly stones

Guided by the star charts, the expeditions push deeper into the jungle until they reach a massive river known among explorers as the Jade Vein.

This immense waterway cuts across the region for miles in both directions. Marching around it would delay an expedition so badly that rivals would almost certainly reach the Jewel first.

The only option is to secure a crossing. At this stretch of the river, an ancient stone bridge still spans the current, though time, flood, and neglect have left it in a dangerous state. One force bold enough to seize it may pass deeper inland. One careless enough to destroy it may lose everything.

Yet the bridge is not the only temptation here. The river has claimed many lives over the years, and on half-submerged stones near the crossing lie the visible remains of earlier attempts: shattered chests, dropped satchels, and valuables stranded just above the torrent.

To reach these prizes, a warband must send one of its boldest companions hopping stone to stone above the rushing water while the rest of the unit waits helplessly on the bank. Sometimes both sides reach the same prize at once, and the fight for it becomes a deadly duel fought on slick, uneven footing.

Thus the battle for the Jade Vein becomes more than a struggle for ground. Expeditions must hold the bridge, guard against disaster, and decide whether greed is worth the lives of their finest warriors.

Scenario III: Scenario Rules

Objectives, rewards, and end conditions

Victory Condition

The player who controls the bridge when the battle ends wins.

If that player's Unit Strength around the bridge is at least double that of the opponent, this counts as a Decisive Victory.

Battlefield Setup

Place a river so that it divides the battlefield into two approximately equal halves. The river should be approximately 12" wide at the crossing point. Place a bridge in the middle of it.

The bridge should be at least 4" wide and 12" long, spanning the full width of the river at that point.

In addition to the river and bridge, place terrain to represent the dense jungle pressing close to the crossing. A good battlefield for this scenario typically includes 1 Ruined Rise, 1-2 Mysterious Jungles, and a small amount of scatter terrain such as broken masonry, abandoned packs, snapped ropes, or half-rotted expedition debris.

Place 2 River Stone markers in the river, one on each side of the bridge. These represent stranded treasure visible above the torrent.

Deployment

Randomly determine which side of the river each player deploys on.

First Turn

Roll off as normal. The player that finished deploying first receives +1 to this roll.

Game Length

At the end of each round, beginning with the fourth, roll a D6 and keep a running total of these rolls. When the total reaches 10 or more, the battle ends.

Controlling the Bridge

To determine which player controls the bridge, calculate the total Unit Strength of all models on the bridge or within 1" of it.

The player whose total is higher (and at least 5) controls the bridge. Any models in the river do not count.

Bridge Collapse

If a player's action causes the bridge to collapse, that player immediately loses the battle (and their opponent earns a Decisive Victory).

River Stones Treasure

Each River Stone marker contains guaranteed treasure.

Each Treasure token recovered from the River Stones grants 1 Loot after the battle.

Scenario III: Terrain Features

The Jade Vein, the bridge, the river stones, and the Ruined Rise

The Jade Vein

The river is effectively impassable terrain to most troops.

Aquatic units, and units with an applicable Strider rule, may move through the river. However, the Jade Vein is so violent that such units gain no protection from Strider while within it.

Any unit capable of moving into or through the river must, when doing so, take a Dangerous Terrain test. These tests are failed on a 1-2. If the unit is moving fast (marching, charging, fleeing, pursuing, overrunning, or making a compulsory move), they instead fail on a 1-3.

A unit that ends its move in the river must immediately take an additional Dangerous Terrain test, even if it already tested earlier that turn.

The Bridge

Units cannot enter the bridge from its sides.

Any units on the bridge have soft cover, as the raised span, broken stonework, and limited angles of fire make it difficult to draw a clear shot.

Collapse Risk

The bridge is ancient and unstable. When any of the following occurs on the bridge, make a Collapse roll:

A war machine hits a unit on the bridge; a template weapon touches the bridge; a Monster ends a charge on the bridge; or a Flying Monster lands on the bridge.

Roll a D6 and add the bridge's current Structural Damage.

On a 1-4, there is no effect. On a 5-6, the bridge weakens and its Structural Damage increases by 1. On a result of 7+, the bridge collapses.

River Stones

A unit in base contact with the riverbank may attempt to claim a River Stone. Only a Companion may move onto the stones. For this purpose, a Companion means a unit champion or a character, including the Expedition Leader.

While a Champion is on the stones, its unit may not move. It is committed to the attempt until the Champion returns or is removed as a casualty.

Deadly Crossing

Each time a Companion moves across the stones, it must take a normal Dangerous Terrain test.

If failed, the Companion is removed as a casualty and any unclaimed treasure remains on the stones.

Duels on the Stones

If opposing Companions meet on the stones, they must issue and accept a challenge.

Models on the stones are treated as being in base contact only with other models on the stones.

Precarious Footing

Models fighting on the River Stones use their Initiative instead of Weapon Skill to determine To Hit rolls.

Any natural roll of 1 To Hit made by a model fighting on the stones requires that model to immediately take a normal Dangerous Terrain test.

Retrieving Treasure

A Companion that reaches a River Stone marker may claim its treasure at the start of their turn.

The treasure must then be carried back to the river bank. Once returned, it is considered to be fully retrieved.

Ruined Rise

The Ruined Rise is a hill crowned with ancient or unusual features. Roll a D6 on the following table to determine what kind of Ruined Rise is present:

D6 Ruined Rise
1 Anvil of Vaul
2 Scree Slope
3 Temple of Skulls
4 Arcane Ruins atop a hill. Replace the usual 6" radius with units in contact with, or atop, the hill.
5 Blessed Bulwark atop a hill. A unit fully atop the hill counts as defending the wall.
6 Acropolis of Heroes atop a hill. Place a building or enclosed walls on the hill; it may be garrisoned. Replace the usual 6" radius with units in contact with, or atop, the hill.

Before Act 2: Battle-Hardened Veterans

The survivors of the early expedition are forever changed

The jungle does not forgive weakness. Beneath its choking canopy, steel is tempered in blood, and only those who endure its trials emerge unbroken. As the expeditions press onward, their warriors are no longer green recruits, but veterans forged in the burning heart of Lustria.

Unit Upgrade

Choose one unit that has taken part in at least two scenarios. That unit gains one of the following:

Veteran
The unit may re-roll any failed Leadership test that is not a Break test.

Stubborn
The unit gains the Stubborn special rule. (Stubborn units are always steadfast, whether or not they have more ranks than their enemy.)

Fall Back!
The first time this unit would be required to take a Break test, it may choose not to. Instead, it immediately Falls Back in Good Order.   • Each surviving unit that includes a Standard Bearer and exits the battlefield scores an additional +25 Victory Points
  • Each slain Expedition Leader grants the opposing player +25 Victory Points

The player with the higher total wins the battle.

This scenario is played in two phases. Each phase uses its own battlefield, representing a different stage of the race.

Game Length

The battle lasts for eight turns unless one player declares Completion earlier.

The Race Forward

A unit that moves off the far edge of a phase has reached that phase's exit.

Tracks represent confined routes such as tunnels, ridge paths, or temple corridors.

Windows Between Tracks

At designated windows, units may draw line of sight into the adjacent track.

Scenario V: Special Rules

Phase transitions, The Gauntlet, completion, and rewards

Phase I to Phase II

At the end of any turn in which a player has one or more units reach the far edge of Phase I, that player's track begins transitioning into Phase II.

All of that player's units that reached the exit that turn are placed at the Phase II starting edge on the following turn, fully within their track.

If there is not enough room to place all such units, any overflow units are treated as if they had the Ambushers special rule, but may only arrive from the marked starting edge of Phase II.

Any units that did not reach the exit of Phase I are also treated this way until they arrive.

This represents faster units and advance elements reaching the final approach before the rest of their force has caught up.

Phase (de)Synchronicity

Each player's track transitions independently. One player may be racing through Phase II while the other is still in Phase I.

The Gauntlet

At the end of Phase II, both tracks converge into a shared final battlefield known as The Gauntlet.

No Defenses may be placed in The Gauntlet. This is the final approach and the last opportunity for the expeditions to delay or deny one another.

The Gauntlet contains two final exits. Units that leave by either of these exits are considered to have completed the race and score Victory Points normally.

Triggered and Area Defenses

Defenses are divided into two broad types:

  • Triggered Defenses activate once when triggered or at a defined moment, then are removed or considered spent
  • Area Defenses remain in play and continue to affect units moving near or through them

Melee Defenses are always treated as Triggered Defenses. They fight one full round of combat when first engaged, then immediately disband or disappear and are removed from play. Ranged Unit Defenses are Area Defenses and may continue firing each turn unless destroyed or otherwise neutralized.

Defenses

Each track contains a number of Defenses, determined by rolling on the Defense Chart.

Defenses are always visible and known to both players.

Defenses may be placed within the track or in adjacent impassable terrain along the edge of the track, if the Defense would reasonably occupy such a position.

Units may not enter impassable terrain in order to attack such Defenses, but they may still shoot at them or otherwise interact with them if able.

Completion

Once a player has successfully exited at least one unit from Phase II, they may choose to declare Completion at the end of their turn.

If they do so, all of their remaining units are immediately removed from the table. Only units that have already exited count toward Victory Points.

The opposing player continues playing out the remainder of the race. The player that declared Completion continues to control any remaining Defenses they placed.

Decisive Victory

If a player declares Completion before their opponent reaches The Gauntlet, and still wins the battle, they achieve a Decisive Victory.

Special Deployment Rules

This scenario overrides deployment rules that would allow units to skip parts of the race.

  • Scouts may not be used
  • Ambushers may not be used except as part of phase transition
  • Any rule that would allow a unit to begin the battle outside its deployment area is ignored
  • Vanguard may only be used if both players agree before the battle

Scenario V: Rules

How to play Locating the Crater

Victory Condition

This battle is a race. Victory is not determined by how many enemy units are destroyed, but by how much of your force successfully reaches the end of the course.

At the end of the battle, each player totals their Victory Points as follows:

  • Each unit that exits the battlefield scores Victory Points equal to its remaining Unit Strength
  • Each surviving unit that includes a Standard Bearer and exits the battlefield scores an additional +25 Victory Points
  • Each slain Expedition Leader grants the opposing player +25 Victory Points

The player with the higher total wins the battle.

Battlefield Setup

This scenario uses a track-based battlefield layout representing narrow tunnels, shattered ridgelines, temple corridors, and unstable chambers.

Each player has their own track, running from one short table edge to the opposite short table edge. Tracks are placed side-by-side and overlap only once in a shared central region known as the Convergence Zone.

Tracks may include branches, choke points, windows, and narrow passages, as shown in the Track Layout page.

Defenses are generated by rolling on the Defense Chart. These are always known to both players and are placed by the opposing player.

Deployment

Each player deploys within 6" of their short table edge, inside their own track.

Units may only deploy within their own track.

First Turn

Roll off as normal. The player that finished deploying first receives +1 to this roll.

Game Length

The battle lasts for six turns.

The Race Forward

A unit that moves off the far edge of its track is considered to have successfully exited the battlefield.

Such units are removed from play and will score Victory Points at the end of the battle as described above.

Track Boundaries

Tracks represent confined routes such as tunnels, ridge paths, or temple corridors.

Units may not leave their assigned track and may never enter the opposing player’s track.

Windows Between Tracks

At designated windows, units may draw line of sight into the adjacent track.

Units may shoot or cast spells across tracks if able, but may never move into the opposing track.

The Convergence Zone

At the center of the battlefield, both tracks merge into a confined and unstable region known as the Convergence Zone.

The Convergence Zone is considered Unstable Ground:

  • Units may not March while within it
  • Units must take Dangerous Terrain tests when charging, fleeing, pursuing, or overrunning within it

Pursuit in the Convergence Zone

When a unit flees within the Convergence Zone, it still flees toward the nearest exit track that does not already contain enemy units, even if this is not directly away from the enemy.

Because this forced movement can create unusual angles, pursuit in the Convergence Zone is resolved abstractly:

  • If the pursuer’s roll is equal to or higher than the fleeing unit’s roll, the fleeing unit is caught as normal
  • Otherwise, do not move the pursuer its full distance. Instead, place it directly behind the fleeing unit, at a distance equal to the difference between the two rolls
  • The pursuer may still not enter the fleeing unit’s track – it may only move as far as the entrance to the track that the fleeing unit is moving along.

This represents the pursuers keeping pace through the cramped passages without being unfairly penalized by the unusual layout of the battlefield.

Scenario VI: The Final Clash

A siege of a dead temple-city over the warpstone crater that holds the Jewel of Lustria

The jungle dies before the city begins.

Not gradually. Not naturally. It simply stops.

No birds. No insects. No sound but the low hum of something that should not exist.

At the center of it all lies the wound.

The rest have come to take it.

But the land itself resists. Flesh prickles. Thoughts fracture. Reality bends and shifts in subtle, terrible ways. Whatever fell here was not meant for this world.

And it will not be claimed without consequence.

Whether the crater held warpstone, relics, or captives worth taking, she meant to return with her holds full.

Factions

Lustria's Guardians

Lizardmen

Lizardmen do not form expeditions in the same manner as the younger races, for they are not trespassers in Lustria but its ancient guardians. Their warbands are more properly temple patrols, relic-recovery parties, sacred hunts, or forces dispatched according to the will of the Slann. While foreign armies race inland, the Lizardmen have already been acting for months, seeking either to secure the comet or contain the disruption it represents to the Great Plan.

Sample Lizardmen Warband: When the comet struck Lustria, the Slann of the temple-cities recognized it at once as an event of profound significance. Skink astrologers compared its fall to ancient plaques, while Saurus patrols were dispatched to cleanse the region of intruders and recover whatever sacred or dangerous force had descended. By the time foreign explorers arrive, the servants of the Old Ones are already watching them.

Amazons

Amazon warbands do not come to Lustria from afar, but they may still converge upon the comet from hidden strongholds, sacred valleys, and jungle settlements. To the Amazons, the fallen star may be a divine sign, a weapon to be denied to invaders, or a threat to be destroyed before it poisons their homeland.

Sample Amazon Warband: Priestess-Captain Tlahua read the comet's fall as a warning from the goddess: a thing of terrible power had struck the heart of the jungle, and already foreigners came swarming like flies to a carcass. With chosen warriors, sacred guardians, and scouts who knew every hidden path, she set out not to enrich herself, but to ensure that no outsider carried the star's power out of Lustria alive.

Amazon Armies: Amazons are native to Lustria and may interact with the campaign differently than most expeditions. If an Amazon army is present and no Lizardmen players are participating, Amazons may use the special Lizardmen scenario notes when applicable, representing them defending sacred sites and jungle territory from invading expeditions.

If both Amazons and Lizardmen are present in the campaign, the Lizardmen retain their role as the primary guardians of Lustria. In this case Amazon forces are treated as an expedition emerging from deeper within the jungle, following the trail of the comet outward toward the coast.

Factions

Servants of Chaos

Warriors of Chaos

Chaos expeditions may be driven by prophecy, mutation, divine command, or the naked desire for a source of immense power. Champions of the Dark Gods see omens everywhere, but a comet of corruption is difficult to misread. Such a force might be sent to claim the star, consecrate it, or simply prove its worth through slaughter.

Sample Chaos Expedition: Among the northern wastes, the champion Kargath Blood-Marked heard the prophets proclaim the comet a sign from the gods. To him, that meant challenge and reward in equal measure. He gathered a brutal band of chosen followers and set sail for Lustria, determined to claim the fallen star and offer its power to the gods—or use it to rise above every rival who had ever doubted him.

Beastmen

Beastmen warbands may be drawn by omens of ruin, the scent of chaos in the air, or the promise of despoiling sacred places. Bray-Shamans interpret falling stars as signs from the dark powers, and a comet rich with corruption would be reason enough to drive an entire herd into frenzy. Their “expeditions” are less journeys than migrations of violence.

Sample Beastmen Expedition: Khorgath the Bray-Shaman howled with delight as the comet burned overhead, for to him it was plainly a sign of favor from the Ruinous Powers. If chaos had fallen into the jungle, then it must be claimed in blood. Gathering a savage herd of gors, ungors, and braying monsters, he led them southward to desecrate whatever the star had touched.

Daemons of Chaos

Daemonic incursions are not expeditions in a mortal sense, but they may follow the same trail for reasons of corruption and manifestation. If the comet carries concentrated warp energy, daemons may be drawn toward it instinctively, or else summoned by cultists who seek to harness its power. Their presence means the barrier between worlds is already fraying.

Sample Daemonic Incursion: When the comet struck Lustria, the shock of its arrival rippled through the Realm of Chaos. In the wake of that disturbance, a hidden cult enacted forbidden rites near the impact zone, tearing open a breach through which daemonic entities emerged. Whether they sought to claim the fallen star or merely revel in the corruption around it mattered little to the mortals in their path.

Factions

The Dead & the Damned

Vampire Counts

Vampiric expeditions may be cloaked in secrecy, conducted through mortal proxies, or led openly by a lord confident enough to ignore the danger. The fallen comet promises magical upheaval, strange death, and relics steeped in power—all things that attract necromancers and undead nobles alike.

Sample Vampire Expedition: Lady Varena felt the comet's arrival as a cold ripple through the winds of death. Something potent had fallen into Lustria—something that might fuel dark rituals for centuries. Rather than march openly, she scattered her agents among a mortal expedition, ensuring that when the time came, the prize would pass quietly into undead hands.

Tomb Kings

Tomb King expeditions are usually ancient duties awakened by omen, prophecy, or arcane necessity. Liche Priests may read celestial signs as part of long-forgotten plans, while kings may seek relics that rightfully belong only in the keeping of Nehekhara. The dead do not race for fortune, but they do march with implacable purpose.

Sample Tomb King Expedition: Deep beneath the sands, Liche Priest Menkhotep studied ancient star-charts that spoke of celestial objects falling in ages past. When the comet appeared, he knew the old prophecies had awakened once more. Summoning a small but disciplined host of skeletal warriors and chariot riders, he sent them across the sea to secure the relic before the living could profane it.

Factions

Ancient Grudges

Dwarfs

Dwarfen expeditions are often built around practical goals: the recovery of star-metal, the reclaiming of ancient lost things, the settling of grudges, or the testing of runic and engineering theories. Dwarfs do not rush into folly lightly, but once convinced that something of value lies in distant lands, they pursue it with stubborn determination.

Sample Dwarfen Expedition: Runesmith Borri Granitehand listened grimly as traders described the comet's fall in distant Lustria. Star-metal of such magnitude could not be ignored. He gathered a small but dependable expedition of warriors, engineers, and loyal clansmen, swearing that no elf, man, or rat would squander such a prize before a proper Dwarf laid claim to it.

Chaos Dwarfs

Chaos Dwarf expeditions are typically driven by industry, prophecy, and cruel ambition. Sorcerer-Prophets, Daemonsmiths, and wealthy overseers alike may seek the fallen comet as a source of rare star-metal, raw warpstone, or arcane fuel for infernal engines. To the Dawi-Zharr, the jungles of Lustria are hostile and alien—but that only makes the prize more valuable.

Sample Chaos Dwarf Expedition: Daemonsmith Zharkhul Flamevein interpreted the comet's fall as both omen and opportunity. If a stone of such power had struck the jungles of Lustria, then it might feed the furnaces of Zharr-Naggrund for years—or elevate its master above every rival in the forges of Hashut. With a small escort of infernal guards, hobgoblin servants, and chained laborers, Zharkhul set sail for the west, determined to drag the Jewel of Lustria back in iron chains.

Orcs & Goblins

Greenskin expeditions are rarely organized in any civilized sense, but they may still form around a great sign, a promising fight, or the rumor of shiny things worth stealing. A comet crashing into the jungle is more than enough to get a warboss moving. Some seek battle, some seek loot, and many seek both without caring much which comes first.

Sample Greenskin Expedition: When the blazing rock tore across the sky, Warboss Grimgut Skullkrakka took it as the clearest sign Gork or possibly Mork had ever sent him. Big sky-rock meant big fight, and big fight meant glory. He gathered his boyz, a pack of lunatic goblins, and as many beasts as could be bullied onto a ship, then headed for Lustria in search of a proper scrap.

Hobgoblins

Hobgoblin expeditions are usually motivated by greed, cunning, and the opportunity to rob stronger fools of their prize. Unlike Orcs, Hobgoblins are quite capable of crossing oceans and striking bargains if profit is to be made. A comet fallen in distant lands promises loot enough to tempt even the most suspicious khan.

Sample Hobgoblin Expedition: Khan Sharptoof One-Eye listened with narrow-eyed interest as foreign traders spoke of a star that had fallen into Lustria. He cared nothing for omens, but plenty for valuables. Gathering wolf riders, sneaks, and a mob of half-starved cutthroats, Sharptoof set out to let the big folk fight over the crater while he stole whatever they were too dead to carry home.

Skaven

Skaven expeditions are acts of greed, rivalry, and religious mania. A fallen star means warpstone, and warpstone means power. Grey Seers, Warlock Engineers, and ambitious Warlords alike might send clawpacks into the jungle, each hoping to seize the God-Stone before rival clans learn the truth. Their ventures are rarely orderly, but they are always desperate.

Sample Skaven Expedition: Grey Seer Skrikch nearly dropped his sacred warpstone when the comet screamed across the sky. A gift from the Horned Rat himself could mean only one thing: a vast shard of divine stone had fallen into Lustria. At once he gathered Clanrats, gutter-runners, and a suspiciously unstable engineer, then ordered them south. If rival clans reached the crater first, he would never survive the shame.

Factions

Monsters & Misfits

Ogre Kingdoms

Ogre expeditions are often blunt in motive but no less compelling for it. A fallen star might be seen as a sign from the Great Maw, a source of unimaginable strength, or simply something vast enough to be worth fighting over. Ogres will chase rumor farther than most if they believe there is food, glory, or plunder at the end of it.

Sample Ogre Expedition: The Firebelly Gutmaw declared that the comet was a sign from the Great Maw itself. If such a thing had fallen into distant lands, then surely it was meant either to be eaten or beaten open. Gathering a mob of hungry ogres, he marched toward the ships bound for Lustria, convinced that the crater would hold riches, battle, and perhaps the greatest meal of his life.

Halflings

Halfling expeditions are unusual, but not unheard of. Some are sponsored by larger powers as scouts, cooks, or quartermasters; others are driven by curiosity, greed, or the simple desire to see what all the fuss is about. A Halfling band in Lustria is likely underestimated by everyone but themselves.

Sample Halfling Expedition: Folco Greenbottle first heard of the fallen comet from a merchant caravan master who swore it would make any man rich. Folco, sensibly, reasoned that there was no reason it should not make a Halfling rich instead. With a small band of archers, burglars, and cousins too curious for their own good, he booked passage westward, fully intending to come home with treasure, stories, and perhaps a few exotic recipes.

Albion

Albionite expeditions are rare, but druids, chieftains, and seers may be moved by portents in the stones and winds. A comet falling far across the sea might be read as a sign of upheaval in the balance of the world. Those who travel from Albion do so less for simple wealth than for prophecy, warning, or sacred duty.

Sample Albionite Expedition: When the standing stones of his homeland groaned beneath a sky split by fire, the druid Mael Brannon knew some great wrong had entered the world. Gathering warriors of the mist-clans and those mad enough to trust his visions, he journeyed south and west toward Lustria. If the fallen star heralded doom, then it must be faced before its corruption spread across sea and stone alike.
